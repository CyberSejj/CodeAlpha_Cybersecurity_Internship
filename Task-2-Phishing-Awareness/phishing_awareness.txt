CODEALPHA - PHISHING AWARENESS TRAINING

1. WHAT IS PHISHING?

Phishing is a social engineering attack in which an attacker
pretends to be a trusted person, company, or service to trick
a victim into revealing sensitive information or performing
an unsafe action.

Common targets include:
- Passwords
- Banking information
- OTPs
- Personal information
- Account credentials


2. COMMON TYPES OF PHISHING

Email Phishing:
Fraudulent emails designed to look like legitimate messages.

Spear Phishing:
A targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific person or organization.

Smishing:
Phishing performed through SMS or text messages.

Vishing:
Phishing performed through phone calls or voice communication.

Social Media Phishing:
Fake messages, profiles, or links used through social media platforms.


3. WARNING SIGNS OF PHISHING

- Unexpected messages
- Urgent or threatening language
- Suspicious sender addresses
- Misspelled words or unusual formatting
- Suspicious links
- Requests for passwords or OTPs
- Unexpected attachments
- Offers that appear too good to be true
- Requests to bypass normal security procedures


4. HOW TO CHECK A SUSPICIOUS MESSAGE

1. Check the sender's email address or phone number.
2. Do not immediately click links.
3. Hover over links on a computer to inspect the destination.
4. Verify the request using an official website or known contact.
5. Check for unusual spelling or grammar.
6. Be suspicious of unexpected attachments.
7. Never share passwords or OTPs through email or messages.


5. SAFE RESPONSE TO PHISHING

If you receive a suspicious message:

- Do not click suspicious links.
- Do not download unexpected attachments.
- Do not provide passwords or OTPs.
- Verify the message through an official channel.
- Report the message as phishing/spam.
- Delete the message after reporting it.


6. PHISHING AWARENESS EXAMPLE

SAFE TRAINING EXAMPLE:

Subject: Urgent Account Verification Required

Example message:

"Your account requires verification. Please review your
account through the official website or official application."

Warning signs to discuss:
- Unexpected request
- Urgency
- Request to verify an account
- Link should be independently verified rather than clicked


7. PHISHING PREVENTION

- Use strong, unique passwords.
- Enable multi-factor authentication.
- Keep software and browsers updated.
- Use security software.
- Verify unexpected requests.
- Learn to identify suspicious links and attachments.
- Report suspected phishing attempts.


8. CONCLUSION

Phishing attacks rely heavily on deception and social engineering.
Users should slow down, verify unexpected requests, avoid suspicious
links and attachments, and report suspected phishing attempts.
